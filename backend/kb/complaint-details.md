# What to tell and what to carry when you complain

## Details to tell when reporting financial fraud
Be ready to tell: your full name, mobile number and address; the date and exact time of the fraud; the amount lost; the transaction ID or UTR number; the bank account, UPI app or card used from your side; the fraudster's bank account number, IFSC, UPI ID, phone number, email or website; how the fraud started (call, SMS, WhatsApp, link, app); what exactly the fraudster said; whether you shared OTP, PIN, card details or installed an app. Give the facts in order of time. Do not guess or exaggerate; say only what you know.

## Documents and evidence to keep ready
Bank statement or SMS showing the debit; transaction receipt or screenshot; screenshots of chats, calls (call log), messages, social media profiles and websites; the fraudster's phone numbers, UPI IDs and account numbers; the APK or app name if you installed one (do not delete it); email copies with full headers if possible; your ID proof such as Aadhaar or Voter ID; passport size photograph if asked; any acknowledgement number from 1930 or the portal. Make two photocopies and keep digital copies.

## How to write a complaint application to the police
Address it to the Station House Officer (SHO) of the police station, mention the subject (for example Complaint regarding online financial fraud), your name and contact number, the date and time of the incident, a clear description in order, the amount lost and the transaction details, the fraudster's details, a request to register an FIR and investigate, a list of enclosures, then sign with the date. Use the Complaint Letter Generator on this portal or ask the assistant to draft a letter. Keep one signed copy with the receiving stamp and date.

## What to say when you call 1930
Say that you are a victim of cyber financial fraud. Give your name, phone number, the amount, date and time, transaction ID, bank or UPI app name and the fraudster's account or phone details. Stay on the line and note the acknowledgement number. Then register on cybercrime.gov.in with the same details.

## What to tell your bank
Say that an unauthorised transaction happened, give the date, time, amount and transaction ID, ask to block the card, UPI or net banking, ask them to put the beneficiary account on hold if possible and give you a complaint reference number. Follow up in writing and keep a copy.

## Complaints about social media harassment, fake profiles or morphed images
Collect screenshots showing the profile name, username, profile link and the offensive content with date and time. Do not delete the conversation. Report the profile to the platform first. Then complain on cybercrime.gov.in and at the police station with the screenshots, the links and your ID proof. Women and children related complaints can be filed anonymously on the national portal.

## If the police do not register your complaint
Politely ask the SHO to register your complaint and give a receipt. You can send the complaint in writing by post to the Superintendent of Police, approach the senior officer, or file the complaint online on cybercrime.gov.in. You can also call the Alwar Cyber Control Room on 8764874306 or Police Emergency 112. Keep copies of everything you submit.

## Mistakes to avoid after a fraud
Do not delay, because delay reduces the chance of freezing the money. Do not delete evidence. Do not share OTP, PIN or card details with anyone claiming to help you recover money. Do not pay fees to people promising refunds. Do not argue with or threaten the fraudster, because they may block you. Do not post personal details on social media.
