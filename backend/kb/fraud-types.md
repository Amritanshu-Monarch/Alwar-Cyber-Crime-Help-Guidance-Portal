# Common cyber frauds and what to do

## UPI, OTP and fake payment request fraud
Fraudsters send a collect request or ask you to scan a QR code or enter your UPI PIN "to receive money". You never need a PIN, OTP or QR scan to receive money. If you shared an OTP or PIN, call 1930 immediately, block your UPI and card from the bank app, and then file a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in. Keep the transaction ID and screenshots.

## Digital arrest scam
Callers pretend to be police, CBI, customs, narcotics or TRAI officers, say your parcel or SIM is linked to a crime, and keep you on a video call claiming you are under digital arrest, then demand money for verification. There is no such thing as digital arrest in Indian law. Police and government agencies never demand money, never ask you to stay on video call and never ask for OTP or bank details. Cut the call, do not transfer money, tell family, and report to 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in. If you already paid, call 1930 at once.

## Fake KYC, bank, electricity or SIM block messages
Messages say your account, SIM or electricity will be blocked unless you click a link, install an APK or call a number. Banks do not ask you to install apps or share OTP by call or SMS. Do not click the link. Report the number on Chakshu at sancharsaathi.gov.in. If you installed an APK, put the phone in airplane mode, call your bank, call 1930 and do not delete the app until police advise.

## Remote access apps such as AnyDesk, TeamViewer and RustDesk
Fraudsters ask you to install a screen sharing app to "fix" a refund, KYC or customer care problem. Once installed they can see your OTPs and control your banking apps. Never install such apps on a caller's request. If you did, uninstall only after taking a screenshot of the app, turn off the internet, change your banking passwords from another device and call 1930.

## Fake customer care number fraud
Fraudsters publish fake customer care numbers on search results and maps. Always use the number printed on the official app, website, card or passbook. A genuine customer care executive never asks for OTP, PIN, CVV or to install an app.

## Job, work from home, task and part time job scams
You are offered easy money for liking videos or completing tasks, are asked to pay a registration or deposit fee, and are shown small initial profits to make you invest more. Real employers do not charge you to hire you. Stop paying, save the chats, payment receipts and the website link, and report to 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in.

## Investment, trading and crypto scams
Scammers add you to WhatsApp or Telegram groups with fake profit screenshots and fake trading apps and promise guaranteed returns. Only invest through SEBI registered platforms. Do not pay more to withdraw money; that is part of the scam. Report with the app name, website, bank accounts you paid and chats.

## Loan app harassment and blackmail
Illegal loan apps take access to your contacts and photos and then threaten to send morphed images to your contacts. Borrow only from RBI regulated lenders. Do not pay extortion. Take screenshots of threats, uninstall the app only after saving evidence, tell your contacts the messages are fake, and report on cybercrime.gov.in and to 1930 if money was paid.

## Sextortion and video call blackmail
Criminals start a friendly chat or video call, record or fake an intimate video and demand money. Do not pay; payment usually leads to more demands. Do not delete the chat, take screenshots of the profile and threats, block and report the profile on the platform, and file a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in where women and child related crimes can be reported anonymously. For intimate images already shared, you can use StopNCII.org to help block their spread on participating platforms.

## Social media account hacking and fake profiles
If your account is hacked, use the platform's account recovery, change the password, turn on two factor authentication, log out of all devices and warn your contacts. If someone creates a fake profile in your name or posts morphed photos, take screenshots with the profile link, report the profile on the platform and file a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in and at the police station.

## ATM, debit and credit card fraud
If your card is misused, block it at once through the bank app or customer care, ask the bank for an unauthorised transaction dispute and report to 1930. At ATMs cover the keypad, check for loose card slots and never accept help from strangers.

## Lottery, gift, prize and customs parcel scams
Messages claim you won a prize or that a gift parcel is held at customs and ask for a fee. You cannot win a lottery you never entered. Do not pay any fee, report the number on Chakshu and keep the messages.

## Online marketplace and QR code scams
A buyer sends a QR code or collect request and says you will receive money. Scanning a QR code or approving a request always sends money out. Receiving money never needs a PIN. Sellers asking for advance payment on fake delivery or army officer stories are also common fraud patterns.

## Aadhaar and identity misuse
Do not share Aadhaar, PAN or OTP with unknown persons. You can lock your Aadhaar biometrics from the UIDAI website or the mAadhaar app and check the authentication history. For Aadhaar problems the UIDAI helpline is 1947. If your identity is used for fraud, file a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in.
